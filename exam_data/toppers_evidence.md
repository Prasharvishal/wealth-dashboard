# Topper Evidence + Verified Exam Structure

Compiled 2026-07-30 from ~60 named selected candidates across UPPSC/BPSC/JPSC.
Every claim carries its source. **Survivorship bias applies** — nobody interviews the
person who followed the same advice and failed. Only *convergent* claims carry signal.

---

## PART 1 — STRUCTURAL CORRECTIONS (these change strategy)

### ⚠️ UPPSC General Hindi (150 marks) IS COUNTED IN MERIT

The widespread coaching claim that it is "qualifying only" is **wrong**. Evidence:
- Official Advt A-1/E-1 Appendix-2 lists it as compulsory paper #1, 150 marks.
- Official note: *"A candidate shall be required to obtain such minimum marks in the paper
  of General Hindi, as may be determined by the Government or the Commission."* — a
  **floor**, not an exclusion.
- **No merit-exclusion clause exists** for it — contrast JPSC/BPSC, which say so explicitly.
- Arithmetic: 150 (Hindi) + 150 (Essay) + 6×200 (GS) = **1500 = the stated Mains total.**
  If Hindi were excluded the total would be 1350. It isn't.

**Syllabus (official, 5 heads):** comprehension of a given passage · संक्षेपण (precis) ·
official/semi-official letters, telegram, office order, notification, circular · word
knowledge (prefix/suffix, antonyms, one-word-for-phrase, spelling & sentence correction) ·
idioms and proverbs.

**There is NO translation (अनुवाद) section** — confirmed absent from the official syllabus
and from the actual 2018 and 2021 papers. Translation belongs to RO/ARO, not PCS.

Observed 2021 paper split (150): comprehension 30 · passage+संक्षेपण 30 · letters 20 ·
antonyms 10 · prefix/suffix 10 · one-word 10 · sentence+spelling correction 10 · idioms 30.

**Medium rules:** General Hindi must be answered in Hindi/Devanagari. **GS papers and Essay
may be answered in English** (English Roman / Hindi Devanagari / Urdu Persian all permitted).
One medium throughout (coaching claim, unverified against the advertisement).

**No subject-wise marks are published by UPPSC** — marksheets are candidate-login-only in
short windows. Any "typical General Hindi marks" figure circulating is unsourced.

### JPSC — two corrections

1. **The Jharkhand-specific paper is PRELIMS Paper 2 (200 marks)**, not a mains paper.
   Prelims = GS-I 200 + Jharkhand-specific 200 = **50% of prelims is home-state content.**
2. **NO NEGATIVE MARKING in JPSC prelims** — official Rules 2021, Jharkhand Gazette
   22-01-2021: *"There will be no negative marking in the Preliminary Examination."*
   Prelims is screening only; marks don't carry to merit.

Mains Paper I (General Hindi 50 + General English 50 = 100) is **qualifying at 30/100 and
explicitly excluded from merit** — gazette verbatim: *"the marks obtained in this paper will
not be counted for the preparation of the merit list."* **Matric standard only.**
Paper II = Language & Literature, 150, **COUNTS in merit**, choose 1 of 15 — **English
Language & Literature is option (v)**. Papers III–VI 200 each. Mains 950 + interview 100 = 1050.
Aggregate qualifying 40% General.

### BPSC — Hindi qualifying, Essay is new and heavy

General Hindi 100 = **qualifying at 30%, NOT in merit** (Commission's own Hindi wording:
*"अंकों का प्रयोग मेरिट निर्धारण में नहीं किया जायेगा"*). Section split: निबंध 30 ·
व्याकरण 30 · वाक्य विन्यास 25 · संक्षेपण 15. **Bihar Board matric standard.**
Merit = GS-I 300 + GS-II 300 + **Essay 300** = 900, + interview 120 = **1020**.
Optional is now **100-mark objective MCQ, also qualifying-only.**

⚠️ **The Essay paper (300 merit marks) was introduced ~2023.** Every topper account from the
64th–69th cycles predates it, and their optional-subject strategies were for a 300-mark
merit-counting descriptive paper that **no longer exists**. Do not transfer those.

## PART 3 — THE SKIP-LIST (highest-value, and thin by nature)

Only a handful of people anywhere state what they left out. All of it:

- **Deepak Singh** (UPPCS 2023 R20, ex-constable): *"One must know what 'not to read'"* and
  concretely — **"UPPCS gives very less focus on medieval history now."**
- **Harsh** (BPSC 66th R48, GS-1 **192**): *"NO need of any study material for GS apart from
  the content being provided by [one source]."* Made notes **only for International Relations.**
- **George Sinha** (JPSC): for Medieval India, **"Old NCERT only."**
- **Amit Gupta** (UPPCS 2019+2020 selected; BPSC selection unverified) — the longest skip list:
  skipped Science prep entirely ("being confident about it"); *"Polity — I skipped as
  Laxmikant was enough"*; *"I never read Mrunal Sir's Economy"*; Lucent's GK — *"I never read
  it!"*; **zero prelims mock tests**; **zero mains test series**; **zero mock interviews**.
- **Apurv** (BPSC 66th R40) — the best framing of the whole problem:
  **"Don't chase excellence, chase optimality. Don't overdo any one section."**
- **Priyangi Mehta** (BPSC 68th **Rank 1**): declined mock interviews entirely; *"I made very
  few diagrams. I did not follow any stereotype in my answer writing... And I still got very
  good marks. Develop your own style."*
- **Love Sharma** (BPSC 64th R183): *"honestly I have not seen previous year paper of BPSC"*
  — recorded as a counter-example, not a recommendation.

**Negative finding: no JPSC topper anywhere gives a syllabus skip quote.** Across ~20 named
JPSC individuals the field is empty.

## PART 5 — ENGLISH MEDIUM: DOES IT WORK?

**Yes, with one caveat.** Hard evidence:
- **BPSC 68th topper answer copies: 7 English / 4 Hindi.** English medium is not a
  disadvantage at BPSC. (69th skewed 8/10 Hindi — varies by cycle.)
- **Monika Srivastava, English medium, BPSC 66th Rank 6, first attempt, while working.**
- UPPSC confirmed English-medium selections: Riddhi Bagaria, Naveen Kumar, Shikhar Mishra,
  Amit Gupta.
- **No credible evidence of systematic scoring penalty** for English answers in any of the three.

**The caveat, from a Rank 6 who lived it** — Monika Srivastava, verbatim:
**"NOT ENOUGH RESOURCES FOR BPSC IN ENGLISH MEDIUM."** The constraint is material
availability for *state-specific* content, not evaluation bias.

**The single most actionable quote for an English-medium candidate**, from Siddharth Gupta
(UPPCS 2023 **Rank 1**), who had scored **poorly in Hindi in his previous attempt and fixed it**:
> *"Dont ignore Hindi paper (especially for English medium candidates). It a low hanging
> fruit. Practice hindi daily."*

Given that UPPSC General Hindi is **150 merit marks** (Part 1), this is the highest-leverage
single item on the list. Pooja Tiwari (UPPCS 2024 R7, SDM, first attempt) prepared it
**from PYQs only** and named no book.

## PART 6 — PRESCRIPTIVE NUMBERS (the only ones anyone gave)

- **Siddharth Gupta** (UPPCS R1): one full-length test weekly early, **2–3 weekly in the
  final 2 months**; **4–5 answers written daily**; **at least 10 essays** before the exam.
- **Amit Gupta**: at least **2–3 full mains tests per paper** before prelims (though he took none).
- **Satvik Srivastava** (UPPCS 2023 R3): **4 hrs/day prelims, 6 hrs/day mains**; complete
  **80% of the mains syllabus before prelims.**
- **Kumar Shubham** (BPSC R213): 5–6 mains mocks. **Love Sharma**: 3–4.
- **Revision counts**: Siddharth Gupta "10–12 revisions"; Amit Gupta "3–4 rounds".
  **No JPSC topper anywhere states a revision count.**

**Convergent methodology across nearly everyone:** PYQ analysis is the backbone
(*"PYQ is the backbone of Civil Services preparation"* — Harsh; *"One can't skip it at any
cost"* — Vijay Pratap; *"PYQ को एनालाइज करें"* — Ananya Trivedi). Limited sources, revised
many times, beats broad collection (*"Keep your sources limited. Timely revision is a must"*
— Divya Sikarwar R1; *"Don't become a Material Collector"* — Amit Gupta).

## PART 7 — WHAT COULD NOT BE FOUND (state plainly, don't fill)

- **No attempts-to-selection statistics** for any of the three exams. No commission publishes it.
- **No success-rate data for working professionals.** No survey exists.
- **No medium-wise selection statistics for JPSC.** Any such claim is unsourced.
- **No full-length-mocks-before-prelims count** from any source.
- **UPPSC publishes no subject-wise marks**, so no verifiable General Hindi score exists.
- The claim "110–115 of 200 JPSC prelims questions are Jharkhand" is **aspirant folklore** —
  JPSC does not publish this split and the official syllabus does not quantify it.
- **YouTube holds most topper booklists and is not text-readable** — the largest single gap.
  biharconnect.in (403) has the richest text 70th-CCE interviews and needs browser access.
